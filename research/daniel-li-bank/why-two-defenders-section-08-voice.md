# Why Defenses Have to Guard Caitlin Clark Twice — Section 8

## Script

Against Minnesota, the same recognition appears over almost the entire court. Clark controls the ball in the backcourt while a Fever runner is already ahead of the nearest recovering defender. Clark releases a one-handed lead pass before Minnesota can build its half-court shell.

The ball travels almost the length of the floor, but the decision is immediate. Clark is not waiting to see whether a conventional half-court action will work. She recognizes that the best opening exists before the action can even begin.

Late in the same game, Clark finds the advantage in a much smaller space. She drives right, the interior defender steps toward the ball, and Mitchell cuts along the baseline. Clark drops the bounce pass into that route, and Mitchell finishes on the reverse side.

One pass covers nearly a full court. The other travels only a few feet. They come from the same habit: use the opening before the defense can erase it. Clark does not need every possession to look the same, because she is not memorizing one answer. She is recognizing the condition that makes an answer available.
