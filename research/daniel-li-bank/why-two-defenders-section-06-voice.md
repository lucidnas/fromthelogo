# Why Defenses Have to Guard Caitlin Clark Twice — Section 6

## Script

Earlier in that Portland game, Clark drives with Kelsey Mitchell moving toward the baseline. Nearby defenders orient and step toward the ball. Mitchell cuts behind that movement, and Clark sends the bounce pass into her path while the opening is still developing.

The pass and the cut arrive at the same place. Mitchell does not have to stop, collect the ball, and restart the possession. She catches in stride and continues to the rim. That timing is the real advantage. Clark releases the pass before the defense can fully settle into the space it is trying to protect.

This is why some of Clark's best passes can look almost too early. She is not throwing to the place where the teammate is standing. She is matching the delivery to the route that is still unfolding. By the time the defense recognizes the destination, the ball and the cutter are already there.
