# Why Defenses Have to Guard Caitlin Clark Twice — Section 9

## Script

The final Portland possession is almost quiet compared with everything before it. Clark controls the ball above the arc. The primary defender stays occupied with her, the help shifts, and Makayla Timpson cuts through the pocket that opens between them.

Clark takes the simple window. She delivers the pass, Timpson catches, and the layup gives Clark her tenth assist and the triple-double. There is no need to force a spectacular pass when the defense has already provided a clean one.

That is the entire problem in one sequence. Clark's range changes where the defense begins. Her drive changes who has to help. Her timing finds the cutter before the opening closes. And her passing angle decides whether the ball can reach that space.

So when a defense guards Caitlin Clark once, it is only solving the first question. Meet her high, and somebody must protect the space behind that decision. Send help, and somebody must cover the cutter. Rotate again, and Clark is already looking at the next window. Every reasonable adjustment has a cost. Clark keeps finding it before the defense can guard her twice.
