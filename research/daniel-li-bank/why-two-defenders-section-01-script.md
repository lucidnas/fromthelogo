# Why Guarding Caitlin Clark With Two Defenders Still Doesn't Work

## Script

Caitlin Clark's range is the first thing every defense has to account for. But the deeper problem is not simply where a defender picks her up. Against Connecticut, the nearest defender is still retreating when Clark recognizes the gap and rises from nearly thirty feet.

Phoenix gives her a different look, but the result is the same. Clark only needs a sliver of space to rise from thirty-one feet. That is why the defense's attention follows her whenever the ball is in her hands, whether she is preparing to shoot or carrying the action into the next read.

Watch this possession against Atlanta. Indiana flows into a drag screen in semi-transition. Clark's defender is trailing the play, so Atlanta's big steps high to keep Clark from turning the corner into a pull-up three.

For a moment, Atlanta has solved the first problem. Two defenders are attached to Clark. But that creates the second one. Aliyah Boston's defender has left the paint, Boston slips into the open lane, and Clark delivers the bounce pass before either defender can recover.

The final pass looks simple because Clark has already made the difficult read. She sees the big step up, knows the space behind her is about to open, and releases the ball before Boston even reaches the middle of the lane.

Minnesota tried to shrink that same space in a different way. Instead of meeting Clark above the screen, they load a second defender toward the nail as she turns the corner. The idea is to stop the drive before Clark can get downhill.

But look at what that extra attention does. Both defenders are looking at Clark, Boston settles into the space directly behind them, and the passing window is already open. Clark threads the ball through the help, and Boston finishes before the defense can rotate back.

The Atlanta coverage and the Minnesota coverage look different, but the geometry is the same. A second defender moves toward Clark, and the defense gives up space behind that movement.

This is the tradeoff every defense faces. Send two defenders toward Clark, and Indiana can play four against three behind them. Stay home on the other four players, and Clark keeps the shooting window for herself. And once the low defender rotates to stop the roller, Clark is already reading the third defender. That is where the problem gets even harder.
