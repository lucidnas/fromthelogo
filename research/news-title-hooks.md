# FTL News Title Hooks — the outlet-derived curiosity-headline library

Canonical title doctrine for the **News Recap** lane. These formulas are reverse-engineered from the
real headlines that SI / Yahoo / Athlon / CBS / Bleacher Report / ClutchPoints ran on Caitlin Clark
/ Fever stories. The news lane competes with these outlets in the feed — match or beat their hook,
don't fall back to the Clark Celebration awe-word formula.

## The core mental model: we are the VIDEO VERSION of their article

Each FTL news video is the **video version of a real outlet article**. Pick the article whose angle
is strongest, then **adopt that angle and the strength of its hook** — do not invent a different
story. The title is essentially their headline, rebuilt as a curiosity-gap YouTube hook. The ONLY
thing FTL changes is the *delivery*: we cover it **more explicitly and more casually** — the
faceless Clark-fan voice, blunter takes, plain-spoken reactions, saying out loud what the outlet
only implies. Same facts, same angle, same hook strength; louder, more direct, more opinionated.

So: solid angle (theirs) + solid hook (curiosity-gap version of theirs) + FTL's explicit/casual
coverage. If the article's angle is weak, pick a different article — don't manufacture an angle.

## The three hard rules (every title)

1. **Curiosity gap — withhold the payoff.** Name that something big/historic/absurd happened; do NOT
   state the stat, number, or punchline. The click *is* the reveal. (Wire/AP/SEO headlines spell out
   the fact — "1st WNBA teammates with 30-point double-doubles" — because they optimize for *search*.
   YouTube optimizes for *curiosity*. Do the opposite of the wire headline.)
2. **Caitlin Clark in the title.** Always. Even when the subject is a teammate or the league.
3. **Strictly factual.** Every word defensible from the receipts. Sensational framing, never invented
   facts. No yellow/awe word (GENIUS, UNREAL, INSANE, etc.) — that pattern is the Celebration lane's.

## The hook formulas (pick one; slot in today's facts)

1. **Withheld Superlative** — name the magnitude, hide the what.
   - Real: SI "Caitlin Clark Adds to **Absurd WNBA Record** in Fever Win Over Sky"
   - Template: "Caitlin Clark Just [Added To / Set] An Absurd WNBA Record No One Saw Coming"

2. **Domination** — "Too Much For [opponent]".
   - Real: SI "Caitlin Clark, Aliyah Boston **Combo Too Much for Sky** in Overtime Win"
   - Template: "Caitlin Clark And [teammate] Were Just Too Much For [opponent]"

3. **Quote-Tease / Reaction** — a reaction verb + a single charged word in quotes.
   - Real: SI "Caitlin Clark **Didn't Hold Back** on the **'Ridiculous'** Call Against Her"
   - Templates: "Caitlin Clark Wasn't Having It After This '[word]' Call" · "Caitlin Clark Didn't Hold Back On [thing]"

4. **Found It / The Missing Piece** — discovery/acquisition.
   - Real: SI "Caitlin Clark **Finds** 3-Point Shot vs Sun"
   - Template: "Caitlin Clark Just Found The One Thing The Fever Were Missing"

5. **Hidden Hero / Secret Behind** — point at an unexpected cause.
   - Real: SI "Sophie Cunningham Is **Unsung Hero Behind** Caitlin Clark Game-Winner" · ClutchPoints "Caitlin Clark **gushes about** genuine chemistry with Aliyah Boston"
   - Template: "The [Real Reason / Secret] Behind Caitlin Clark's [result] Isn't What You Think"

6. **The Label / New Status** — coin or quote a nickname/role.
   - Real: SI "Caitlin Clark's **New Status**, Sophie Cunningham as **'Swiss Army Knife'**"
   - Template: "Caitlin Clark Just Earned A New Title — And It's Not What You'd Guess"

7. **Authority / Villain Action** — the league/refs act on Clark.
   - Real: Athlon "**WNBA to Punish** Caitlin Clark After Indiana Fever Game"
   - Templates: "The WNBA Just Put Caitlin Clark On The Clock" · "The Refs Just Made Caitlin Clark's Season Harder"

8. **Prophecy / Open Loop** — a near-future reveal or a "just changed everything".
   - Real: SI "**We'll Know What the Indiana Fever Are Very Soon**"
   - Template: "Caitlin Clark Just Changed The Fever's Biggest Question"

9. **Just Ask / Simple Answer** — withhold the answer, point to Clark.
   - Real: SI "The Way Forward for Fever Is Simple—**Just Ask Caitlin Clark**"
   - Template: "The Fever's Fix Was Simple — Just Ask Caitlin Clark"

## Self-check before locking a title

- Could a wire service have written it verbatim? If yes, it spells out the fact — rewrite to withhold.
- Does it open a loop the video pays off? If not, it's a label, not a hook.
- Is "Caitlin Clark" in it, and is every claim true to the receipts? If not, fix.
- Read it cold: do you *need* to click to learn the what? If you already know the what, the gap is missing.
